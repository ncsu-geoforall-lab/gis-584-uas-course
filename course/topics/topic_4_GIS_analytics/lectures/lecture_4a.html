Ground control point errors

  • Both flights fit their control to about 2 cm and an eighth of a pixel
  • Same markers, same surveyed coordinates, so the tables are directly comparable
  • These are control errors: they say how well the model was forced onto the targets
  • 3B: control error overstates accuracy 3 to 8 x; checkpoints give the honest number

Reading one row of the GCP table

  • Error (m): distance between the surveyed and the estimated marker position
  • Projections: how many photos the marker was placed on
  • Error (pix): RMS reprojection error of the marker over those photos
  • Meters tells you about the georeferencing; pixels tells you about the marker placement
  • A marker with large pixel error and few projections was probably misplaced on a photo
  • Fix: go back to Stage 2 of 2B and re-place that marker

Camera calibration

During alignment the software estimates both the exterior orientation of every photo and the interior orientation of the camera, including nonlinear lens distortion. The adjusted values are in Tools > Camera Calibration and on the report’s calibration page.

  • Interior orientation from 2A: focal length, principal point, distortion
  • Self-calibration: these are solved from the photos, not measured in a lab
  • Camera type must be right first: Frame for the NEX-5, Fisheye for action cameras

Reading the calibration parameters

Parameter Meaning
Fx, Fy Focal length in pixels along each axis
Cx, Cy Principal point: where the optical axis meets the sensor
Skew Non-orthogonality of the pixel axes (near zero)
K1 to K4 Radial distortion coefficients
P1, P2 Tangential distortion coefficients
  • Cx, Cy should sit near the sensor centre: 2456 by 1632 for this 4912 by 3264 sensor
  • K1 dominates radial distortion; K3 and K4 absorb what is left and can overfit
  • Large or unstable values between flights of the same camera are a warning sign
  • 3B: a bad lens model is the mechanism behind doming

Image residuals

  • The calibration page’s second figure: residual vectors over the sensor
  • Each vector is averaged over all photos for that part of the frame
  • Pattern to look for: small and random in the centre, growing toward the corners
  • Systematic swirls or a one-sided pattern mean the lens model is wrong

What a residual is

Image residual: the distance in pixels between where the calibration model projects a 3D point onto the photo and where that point was actually measured in the photo

  • Two positions for the same point on the sensor: predicted and observed
  • The vector between them is the residual; the plot averages them per sensor cell
  • 1 pixel scale bar: these are sub-pixel quantities

Reprojection error

Reprojection error: the RMS image residual over all tie points, the number the report quotes in pixels; it is the same quantity the photogrammetric literature calls the RMS image residual (Agisoft LLC 2025)

  • The report prints one number for the whole project; the plot shows its spatial structure
  • Low reprojection error says the model is internally consistent
  • It says nothing about where the model sits on the ground: that is the GCP and checkpoint tables
  • Nouwakpo 2016 is the reading on how residuals and calibration drive accuracy (Nouwakpo et al. 2016)

The same flight in three programs

  • With GCPs, three programs agree on the mean elevation to within a meter
  • Without GCPs, the two programs disagree with each other by 35 m and with the truth by 36 to 71 m
  • With control, software choice is a second-order effect: the means differ by less than a meter
  • Without control, every program lands wherever the navigation-grade geotags put it

With and without ground control

The same photos processed with no GCPs and with 11 GCPs. The shape is identical; the elevation is off by about 30 m without control.

  • A pure vertical offset: the relative model is fine, the absolute position is not
  • This is the geotag altitude error from 2A and 3B, usually tens of meters on consumer GNSS
  • One GCP, or an RTK aircraft, removes most of it (3B, Zhang 2019)
  • Not every GCP-free error is a shift: the next section is the one that bends

The bowl effect

  • Section 4: the systematic error that control fixes and differencing cannot forgive
  • 3B introduced it as doming; here is what it looks like in a real project

The bowl effect

Bowl (or doming) effect: a smooth, broad bending of the whole model introduced during alignment when the camera calibration is estimated inaccurately; the self-calibration trades lens distortion against a curved surface (James and Robson 2014)

  • The difference between a DSM processed without GCPs and one with: a dome tens of meters high
  • Nothing on the ground is shaped like this; it is the lens model gone wrong
  • A mean error far from zero in a report is the detector (3B’s bias rule)
  • Parallel nadir photos of flat terrain are the classic trigger: James and Robson 2014

Avoiding the bowl

  • Use GCPs spread across the block, or RTK/PPK camera positions, and optimize on them
  • Improve the camera calibration: a known camera model, or convergent and oblique photos so the lens can be solved
  • Increase the overlap and add cross strips so every point is seen from more angles
  • Post-process: fit and remove the dome against control, a last resort that treats the symptom
  • Order of power from 3B: known calibration, oblique imagery, two heights, then control
  • Overlap alone does not fix doming (James 2020); it helps only when it adds view angles
  • The field recipe: tilt the camera 20 to 35 degrees for part of the flight and fly an orbit
  • Post-processing is what you do when the flight is over and cannot be reflown

Optimization

Optimize on the camera geotags and the markers sit 36 m off. Optimize on the markers and the cameras sit 37 m off. The ground control wins.

  • The same project optimized two ways; the 36 m is the geotag altitude bias
  • Whichever reference you weight, the other reports the disagreement
  • 2B Stage 2: first on cameras to get close, then on markers to get right
  • 3B: the Reference Settings accuracies decide how much each reference is trusted

What optimization does

  • A full photogrammetric bundle adjustment with the ground control as additional constraints
  • Exterior orientation of every camera and the interior orientation of the lens are re-solved together
  • Replaces the simple seven-parameter transform (shift, rotation, scale) that georeferencing uses by default
  • The reported errors drop by orders of magnitude when the control is good

Marker accuracy in the Reference Settings is the weight: 5 mm says the targets are trusted to 5 mm. Wrong weights give a wrong adjustment (Agisoft LLC 2025)

  • Without optimization the model is only moved, rotated, and scaled onto the GCPs; the bowl survives
  • With it, the lens model is refit under the constraint that the GCPs are where they were surveyed
  • That is why the bowl disappears with control and not with a plain georeference
  • Lab: the DSM without GCPs is exactly the unoptimized case

Measuring on the products

  • Section 5: what the lab does with the products

Profiles in Metashape

Draw a polyline on the DSM or model and read the elevation profile along it.

  • Measure Shape: Planar, Profile, Volume tabs
  • Profile over a brush pile: 2 to 3 m above the field in the DSM
  • Compare the DSM profile with the same line in GRASS (r.profile) in the lab

Contours in Metashape

Tools > DEM > Generate Contours. Contours are shapes, so they export as vector files alongside the DEM.

  • February 2018 project: 507 cameras, 7 markers, 15 cm DEM, 3.75 cm orthomosaic
  • Contours on a DSM follow the canopy as well as the ground; look at the tree lines
  • Bare winter fields make this flight the better one for terrain contours

Volumes and areas in Metashape

Close a polygon around the pile, pick the base plane, and read the volume above and below it. The model must be watertight under the polygon.

  • Volume needs a base: best fit plane, mean level, or a custom plane
  • Holes under the polygon make the number wrong; this is where Close Holes (2B) matters
  • Lab: measure the same pile twice with different bases and report the difference
  • 3B: the volume uncertainty follows from sigma-z times the footprint area

r.relief for the hillshade; curvatures from r.slope.aspect or from the interpolation itself (v.surf.rst).

  • Curvature amplifies the smallest surface detail, including the error
  • Shaded relief is the quickest visual check of a DSM: artifacts jump out
  • The DSM without GCPs would show the bowl as a slow gradient in slope and aspect

Advanced terrain analysis

Local relief

Sky-view factor

Addons r.local.relief and r.skyview; install with g.extension.

  • Local relief removes the broad trend and leaves the micro-topography: furrows, rows, tracks
  • Skyview factor is independent of light direction, unlike a hillshade
  • Both are tools for subtle features on bare ground, which is where UAS DSMs excel
  • 4B does the same analysis on lidar surfaces

Key terms

Processing report: the record of images, overlap, calibration, and errors from a run;

Image residual: pixel distance between a point projected through the camera model and its measured position

Reprojection error: the RMS of the image residuals over all tie points

Bowl effect: broad systematic bending of the model from an inaccurate self-calibration; also called doming

Optimization: the bundle adjustment re-run with ground control as constraints

DSM difference: between repeat flights it measures error; between epochs, change plus error

  • Midterm vocabulary, and the words the lab report has to use correctly
  • Control error versus camera location error: neither one is the map accuracy
